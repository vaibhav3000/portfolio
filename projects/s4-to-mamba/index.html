01/Overview

What this is.

ssmbench is a from-scratch experimental study of how efficient sequence models evolved: a causal Transformer baseline alongside S4D, Mamba, Mamba-2 and Mamba-3, all implemented in pure PyTorch behind one shared training skeleton, then trained and benchmarked on three designed tasks plus a 1K-32K token efficiency sweep on a single 6 GB RTX 4050.

Every architecture shares the same block plumbing, so a benchmark difference isolates the mixer, not the scaffolding. Every number is committed as JSON with its environment block, and every dual compute form (FFT convolution vs recurrent step, chunked vs sequential scan) is paired with an equivalence test at 1e-3 tolerance.

02/Problem

What motivated it.

Attention scales quadratically with sequence length, and long context is where it becomes unusable. State-space models promise O(T) computation with a fixed-size recurrent state - but each generation of SSM architecture carries its own trade-off, and the claims are easy to repeat and hard to verify.

The gap this project attacks: what survives when you implement each generation from first principles, train the models at matched budgets, and measure the claims yourself - including the wall-clock cost that operation-count notation hides.

03/System

How it is built.

transformer_minimal.py
Pre-norm causal Transformer with SDPA attention; the quality and scaling baseline. No KV cache: decode recomputes the full prefix, which the efficiency sweep measures.
s4_minimal.py
S4D: complex diagonal A with S4D-Lin initialization, exact ZOH via a numerically safe (e^z-1)/z, FFT-convolution training form and an O(1)-state recurrent decode step.
mamba_minimal.py
Mamba-1: input-dependent Δ, B, C (selectivity) as an explicit time-loop selective scan, with a verified incremental step() for decoding.
mamba2_minimal.py
Scalar-per-head A reduces the recurrence to SSD: chunked decayed-attention matmuls with a carried (N, P) state, plus a sequential_scan reference used by the equivalence tests.
mamba3_minimal.py
Complex diagonal transitions A = -exp(a) + iθ with a data-dependent exponential-trapezoidal rule; reduces exactly to Mamba-1/2 when λ = 1.
shared skeleton
blocks.py (classifier heads), synthetic + IMDb data pipelines, a config-driven trainer that emits JSON, and a CUDA-event efficiency harness.

04/Approach

The mechanisms that matter.

  • Math-verified discretization

    Exact ZOH with a Taylor fallback keeps (e^z-1)/z stable for tiny z. The recurrence is asserted against its parallel form, never eyeballed.

  • Chunked SSD with a sequential reference

    Mamba-2's decayed-attention form is computed block-wise in chunk groups to bound transient memory (about 1 GB saved at 32K), and tested equivalent to the naive sequential scan for aligned (32) and misaligned (64) chunk sizes at atol/rtol 1e-3.

  • Complex transitions for state tracking

    Mamba-3 pairs A = -exp(a) + iθ with a data-dependent trapezoidal λ. The rotation phase is what lets the state track parity; a test pins the reduction to exponential-Euler when λ = 1, θ = 0.

  • OOM as data

    The efficiency sweep records out-of-memory points as first-class results (oom_train: true) instead of hiding them, including the WDDM shared-memory spill past the 6 GB card.

  • Slow on purpose

    The selective scans are plain Python loops. That turns the gap between O(T) operation counts and real GPU execution into a measured, teachable finding.

05/Evaluation

Setup and workloads.

  • Hardware: RTX 4050 Laptop (6 GB), CUDA 13; seed 42 for every run; all numbers committed under results/ with environment blocks.
  • Tasks: cumulative-XOR parity (L=128), selective copying (train L=64, test L=128), IMDb sentiment (12.5k samples, max_len 512), each at matched training budgets per config.
  • Efficiency sweep: CUDA-event timing, 5 repeats, batch 8, d_model 256 / 2 layers, sequence lengths 1K to 32K; per-model training-step latency, peak memory, throughput and decode cost, with the Python-loop models capped at 4K.

07/Findings

What the results say.

  1. 01

    Complex input-dependent transitions are what restore state tracking: Mamba-3 is the only model above chance on cumulative parity (0.983 vs 0.51-0.56).

  2. 02

    LTI fails selectively even when complex: S4D's fixed rotation cannot encode per-bit rotations, collapsing to 6.4% on selective copying.

  3. 03

    Ranking flips with the task: selective SSMs win selective copying, but the Transformer and S4D win exact copying in distribution (1.00 at L=32) - the task formulation, not the architecture, decides.

  4. 04

    O(T) operations are not fast wall-clock: the Python-loop selective scan is 87x slower than S4D's FFT at 1K (2 632 ms vs 19.5 ms) - kernel launches and autograd dominate.

  5. 05

    The absolute-PE Transformer reproduces the published copying failure exactly: 1.00 in distribution at L=32, 0.036 at L=64.

08/Decisions

Why it was built this way.

  • One skeleton, five mixers

    Every backbone shares blocks.py, so a benchmark difference isolates the mixer rather than the plumbing.

  • Slow scans on purpose

    Keeping the selective scan as a Python loop turns the theory-vs-wall-clock gap into a measured, teachable result.

  • Verify math, not shapes

    Each dual compute form has an equivalence test at atol/rtol 1e-3: FFT vs recurrent, chunked vs sequential, and the λ=1 reduction.

  • Chunk-grouped SSD

    Processing decay matrices in bounded groups cut roughly 1 GB of transient memory at 32K.

  • Honest failures

    Full S4 (NPLR), fused kernels and Mamba-3's MIMO form are declared not-implemented; OOM is recorded as data, not a crash.

09/Validation

How the claims are checked.

  • A 14-test suite (tests/test_models.py) asserts S4D's FFT-convolution equals its recurrent step; Mamba-2's chunked scan equals the sequential reference for aligned (32) and misaligned (64) chunk sizes at atol/rtol 1e-3; Mamba-3 reduces to exponential-Euler exactly when λ=1, θ=0; and Mamba's step() equals a full forward.
  • Dataset contract tests pin task semantics: parity is cumulative XOR, selective-copy masking, exact-copy BOS/SEP/EOS structure.
  • Every benchmark JSON embeds its environment block (GPU, torch, CUDA version) and the seed, so numbers are attributable.

10/Limitations

Stated plainly.

  • Single seed (42) on one 6 GB laptop GPU: rankings are measured, not statistical.
  • No fused CUDA/Triton kernels: wall-clock results reflect the Python-loop implementations by design.
  • Full S4 (NPLR/Cauchy) is not implemented - S4D only; Mamba-3's MIMO form and chunked dual are documented but not implemented.
  • IMDb skips Mamba-1/3 on wall-clock budget; no language-modeling/perplexity evaluation.
  • Near-OOM timings on Windows are distorted by WDDM shared-memory spill (documented in the README).

Likely questions

  1. Q01Why does ZOH produce (e^z-1)/z, and how do you keep it stable?

    Exact ZOH for diagonal A gives B-bar = ΔB(e^z-1)/z with z = Δa. For |z| < 1e-4 the code switches to a Taylor series (1 + z/2 + z²/6) to avoid 0/0.

  2. Q02Why can't LTI models do parity?

    Cumulative parity needs input-dependent state rotations. S4D is complex but its rotation is fixed; real-transition eigenvalues cannot rotate at all. Only input-dependent complex transitions (Mamba-3) encode per-bit rotations.

  3. Q03How do you prove chunked SSD equals the sequential scan?

    A test runs both forms on identical inputs and asserts allclose at atol/rtol 1e-3, for aligned (32) and misaligned (64) chunk sizes.

  4. Q04If Mamba is O(T), why is it 87x slower than S4D?

    Operation count is not wall-clock. The Python-loop scan pays per-step kernel launches and autograd graph overhead, while S4D trains as one FFT convolution.

  5. Q05What happens to the Transformer at 32K?

    30 ms becomes 186 s per training step with ~9.9 GB peak memory (via WDDM spill): the quadratic term dominates, while O(T)-state models hold longer or OOM on the 6 GB card.
